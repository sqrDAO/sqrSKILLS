# Key Terms Glossary

> UNVERIFIED except for specifically dated, cited propositions. Check current primary
> sources and transaction facts before relying on legal or market assertions.

Definitions and illustrative negotiating ranges. The ranges are not verified
market benchmarks; source current comparisons before labeling a term market or
off-market. Always show the math when a term affects money.

## Equity-leg economics

**Post-money valuation cap.** The maximum company valuation at which a SAFE or
note converts, measured after all the round's SAFE money is counted. Investor's
implied ownership is roughly Purchase Amount divided by the cap. A $50k check at
a $4M post-money cap implies about 1.25 percent. This is an arithmetic illustration, not evidence of a market median.

**Discount.** A percentage reduction off the priced-round price per share that
the SAFE or note holder pays. Typical: 20 percent (investor pays 80 percent of
the round price). Most post-money SAFEs are cap-only with no discount; notes more
often carry a discount.

**MFN (most favored nation).** If the company later issues a convertible on
better terms (lower cap, higher discount), this holder may elect those terms. In
token instruments, an MFN-style clause can extend to a later SAFT / TPA with
better token terms.

**Pro-rata right.** The right to invest in future rounds to maintain ownership
percentage. Removed as a default in the YC post-money SAFE; restored via a
separate Pro Rata Side Letter. In token deals, expressed as a right to maintain
token ownership percentage in later sales.

**Interest rate (notes only).** Annual rate accruing on a convertible note or
bond until conversion or maturity. Common: 5 to 8 percent. Usually converts to
equity alongside principal rather than being paid in cash.

**Maturity (notes only).** The date the note is due if it has not converted.
Common: 18 to 24 months. On maturity the principal plus interest is repayable
unless converted or extended.

**Liquidation priority.** Where the instrument sits on a liquidity or
dissolution event. A SAFE typically sits like standard preferred for the
cash-out; a note sits as debt (senior to equity). Confirm the stack.

**Cash-out amount.** On a liquidity event, the return of the Purchase Amount as
the floor, versus the as-converted common value; the investor takes the greater.

## Token-leg economics

**Reference price per token.** The agreed price used to size a token allocation
(Purchase Amount divided by reference price equals token count). Compare against
any known trading or IDO price to gauge the effective discount.

**Token allocation basis.** How the investor's token count is defined:
(a) fixed formula (Investment Amount divided by a set token price), or
(b) pro-rata proportion of the company's or contributors' token allocation on a
fully diluted, as-converted basis. Pin down which one; they behave very
differently if supply or valuation changes.

**Total token supply and tokenomics table.** The fully diluted token count and
its allocation across team, investors, ecosystem, community, liquidity, and
reserve. Every percentage-based term references this. Watch for tokenomics-change
consent rights and pro-rata upgrade-on-supply-change clauses.

**TGE (Token Generation Event) / Token Launch.** The point at which tokens are
first created and issued to non-insiders. Triggers warrant exercise and SAFT
delivery. Define it precisely; vague TGE definitions cause disputes.

**Lockup.** A period after Token Launch during which tokens cannot be
transferred. Common: 6 to 18 months. Distinct from vesting.

**Vesting and cliff.** The schedule over which locked tokens (or shares) release.
Common token pattern: a small unlock at TGE (for example 5 percent), a short
cliff (for example 2 months), then linear monthly vesting over 16 to 24 months.
Advisor token vesting often runs longer (for example 48 months).

**Anti-dilution / tokenomics-upgrade protection.** If the issuer later sells
tokens below the investor's reference price, or upgrades supply, the investor
gets additional tokens to preserve effective price or percentage. Confirm whether
it is full-ratchet or pro-rata and whether it survives vesting.

**Refund on dissolution.** In a SAFT or token term sheet, a defined refund
(often 100 percent of the Purchase Amount, less taxes and expenses) if the
project dissolves before delivery. Investor-protective; confirm it is present and
the priority.

## Control and information terms

**ROFR (right of first refusal).** The right to match or exceed a competing offer,
often over the founder's next project or token.

**ROFO (right of first offer).** The right to be offered participation in a future
raise first, commonly to maintain token or equity ownership percentage.

**Information rights.** The right to receive periodic updates on development,
finances, tokenomics changes, and future sales. Standard for leads.

**Tokenomics-change consent.** A requirement that the issuer obtain investor
consent (or the lead's consent) before material changes to token supply,
mechanics, or utilities. Strong investor protection; founders resist broad
versions.

**Market-making token loan.** A separate arrangement (documented in its own Token
Loan Agreement) where the issuer lends tokens to an investor or market maker for
liquidity, at a strike price. Keep it legally independent of the side letter.

## Framing and legal terms

**Non-security / utility-token framing.** Fact-supported purchaser representations and risk
acknowledgments under the applicable test. They do not bind regulators or
eliminate exposure; avoid settled classification assertions. See `regulatory.md`.

**Legal-opinion covenant.** An undertaking (common in Singapore-linked token
deals) to deliver, within a set period, a counsel opinion on compliance with
local law (for example the Payment Services Act, Securities and Futures Act, and
any gambling or gaming law).

**Binding arbitration clause.** Dispute resolution by arbitration at a named seat
(for example Singapore / SIAC). Standard in TPAs. Note it affects the investor's
litigation rights.

**Governing law.** The law that governs the document. Match it to the entity and
parties; do not leave it inconsistent across the equity and token legs of the
same deal.
