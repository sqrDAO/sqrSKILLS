# The 8-Stage Sales Cycle

Stages are not activities. A stage is a claim about what the *buyer* has done, and it must be falsifiable. "Sent proposal" is an activity and tells you nothing. "Buyer confirmed the proposal is being routed to legal, and named the legal contact" is a stage.

For each stage, define three things: **entry evidence** (what the buyer did to get here), **the goal** of the stage, and **exit criteria** (what must be true to advance). Deals that cannot meet exit criteria go backwards, not forwards. A pipeline that only moves right is a pipeline that lies.

---

## Stage 1. Discovery / Qualification Call

*Goal:* Establish pain and whether the account can plausibly buy.

*Run:* PNUB. Pain first. Spend 70% of the call listening. The most common failure is demoing.

*Exit:* Pain named and owned by a person. A rough sense of size and timeline. Agreed next meeting on the calendar, with a named attendee list. If you cannot get a next meeting booked live on this call, the deal is already sick.

## Stage 2. Further Discovery / Alignment Call

*Goal:* Widen from one person's pain to the organization's initiative.

*Run:* Map the initiative and the stakeholders. Establish the decision maker and purse holder, or the route to them. Get the buyer's number for the cost of the pain.

*Exit:* Initiative named. Decision maker and purse holder identified. Buyer has stated an impact figure.

## Stage 3. Alignment Call

*Goal:* Agree on what a successful evaluation would look like before you evaluate anything.

*Run:* Co-author the success criteria. Ask directly: "If the pilot shows X, what happens next, and who has to be in the room?" Get the answer in writing, even if the writing is your own recap email that they confirm.

*Exit:* Written, mutual success criteria. Named evaluation participants. Confirmed decision process and rough dates.

## Stage 4. POC

*Goal:* Prove the criteria from Stage 3. Nothing else.

*Run:* Time-boxed, scope-locked, with a defined measurement and a defined owner on the buyer's side. An open-ended POC is a free consulting engagement with an unpaid invoice at the end.

*Exit:* Criteria met or not met, measured, and the buyer says so out loud. A POC that "went well" without a stated result has not exited this stage.

## Stage 5. Objection Handling / Further Alignment / Price Check

*Goal:* Surface every objection before the paper exists.

*Run:* Ask for objections rather than waiting for them. "What is the strongest argument someone in your company will make against doing this?" Give a price range now. Discovering a 10x price mismatch at proposal is an avoidable, self-inflicted loss.

*Exit:* Objections logged with owners. Price range acknowledged without shock. Competition named, including the do-nothing option.

## Stage 6. Initial Proposal / Objection Handling / Negotiation

*Goal:* A proposal the champion can defend when you are not in the room.

*Run:* The proposal restates the buyer's pain, their impact figure, the Stage 3 criteria, and the POC result, then prices against that. Negotiate on scope and terms before price. Every discount buys something: a case study, a multi-year term, a reference call, a faster close.

*Exit:* Proposal reviewed with the economic buyer present. Open items are commercial, not conceptual.

## Stage 7. Final Proposal / Verbal Commit

*Goal:* Verbal yes plus a mapped paper process.

*Run:* Ask the question plainly: "Is there any reason we would not proceed?" Then map security review, legal, procurement, and vendor onboarding with dates and names. This stage is where forecasts die quietly.

*Exit:* Verbal commit from the economic buyer. Paper process mapped with a named contact per step and a target signature date.

## Stage 8. Won / Lost

*Goal:* Learn.

*Run:* Every closed deal, both directions, gets a written reason coded to one cause: no pain, no urgency, no budget, lost to competitor, lost to do-nothing, lost on product gap, lost on trust. Ask the buyer. Losers answer honestly more often than sellers expect, because the pressure is off.

*Exit:* Reason coded. If won: handoff to delivery with the Stage 3 success criteria attached, because those criteria are now the renewal criteria.

---

## Health checks

- **Stage skew.** If most of the pipeline sits in Stages 5 and 6, the qualification in Stages 1 and 2 is too soft.
- **Ageing.** Set a maximum age per stage. Anything older is regressed or closed-lost, not "still active."
- **The single-threaded deal.** One contact, no matter how enthusiastic, is not a deal. Champions leave.
- **Seller-defined stages.** If any stage's exit criterion describes something *you* did rather than something *they* did, rewrite it.

## Filling the workbook tab

Under each of the eight stage headers, write your own entry evidence, goal, and exit criteria. Then add two numbers you will track per stage: **conversion rate to next stage** and **median days in stage**. Those two, per stage, are the entire diagnostic apparatus of a sales motion.
