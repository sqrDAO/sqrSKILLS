# Partnership Goals Matrix

For BD and partnership motions, especially in Web3 and platform businesses, where growth comes through a counterparty rather than from a buyer. The unit of planning is not the partner. It is the **goal**, and the partners are the means.

Teams get this backwards constantly. They open a spreadsheet, list forty logos they admire, and then reverse-engineer a reason to talk to each one. The result is a lot of calls and no attributable outcome. Start from what must be true this quarter, then ask who can make it true.

## The matrix

One row per goal-and-partner-segment pair. Columns:

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| **No** | Row id |
| **Goal Type** | The outcome category. Repeat the goal across rows; leave blank on continuation rows for readability. |
| **Target Metrics** | The counted number that proves the goal moved. Not "awareness." |
| **Breakdown on target partners** | The *segment*, not the logo. "DeFi protocols on Berachain," not "Beraborrow." Logos go in a separate pipeline sheet. |
| **Offer** | What the partner gets. If this is blank, you do not have a partnership, you have a request. |
| **Est Timeline** | Target window for the outcome, not for the first call. |
| **Resources Required** | Engineering weeks, incentive budget, marketing spend, headcount. Be specific. This column is what makes the plan a plan rather than a wish. |
| **Priority** | P0 / P1 / P2. Enforce a limit on P0. |
| **PIC** | Person in charge. One name. Not a team. |
| **Update / end-results** | Written as a completed sentence, in past tense, as if the quarter is over. This is the single most useful discipline in the sheet. See below. |
| **Note** | Anything else. |

## The end-results discipline

Fill the results column *before* you start, in past tense, with the numbers you intend. "Connected with 5 projects, total deposit of $X TVL." "Closed deals with 3 agencies, brought in 12 KOLs." "Hosted 4 AMAs, generated 20,000 views and 1,500 new members."

Two things happen. Vague goals become impossible to write in past tense, which exposes them immediately. And at quarter end you are comparing a sentence to a sentence rather than arguing about whether the partnership "went well."

## Standard goal types

These recur across most Web3 protocol partnership plans. Use them as a starting menu, not a template to copy.

**TVL growth.** Metric: total value locked, in dollars. Partner segments: DeFi protocols on the same chain, yield aggregators, whales and treasuries. Offers differ sharply by segment: protocols want integration support and co-incentives; aggregators want routing volume and fee share; whales want a KYC-clean structure and a durable, long-horizon benefit rather than a farm-and-dump incentive. Be honest with yourself about which of your TVL is rented.

**Branding / awareness.** Metric: social metrics, follower growth, reach attributable to top KOLs and partner projects. Partner segments: agencies, events and AMAs, DeFi builders. Offers: stablecoin fees, distribution through your channels, co-branded activities, KOL involvement. This is the goal type most likely to have an unfalsifiable metric. Force a denominator: reach per dollar, or new followers who took an on-chain action.

**Integration.** Metric: number of shipped integrations, or case studies published. Partner segments: projects technically able to integrate now. Offers: joint announcement, KOL involvement, engineering support. Note that engineering support belongs in Resources Required and is nearly always underestimated.

**User acquisition.** Metric: DAU/MAU, or retained wallets at day 30. Partner segments: communities, KOLs, projects with a large existing user base, education and news sites. Offers: airdrops, distribution, co-run user activities, stablecoin fees. Airdrop-sourced users decay fast; measure at day 30, not day 1, or you are measuring your own incentive budget.

**Launchpad.** Metric: deals closed. Offer: launch fee percentage, stablecoin retainer.

## Sanity checks before the plan ships

1. **Offer symmetry.** Every row has something in the Offer column. A row where you receive TVL and the partner receives "exposure" will not close.
2. **Metric falsifiability.** Every Target Metric could come back as a disappointing number. If it cannot, it is not a metric.
3. **Resource honesty.** Sum the Resources Required column. Compare it to what the team actually has. Most partnership plans are overcommitted by a factor of two or three, and the correction is dropping rows, not working harder.
4. **PIC uniqueness.** No person is PIC on more than three P0 rows.
5. **Segment, not logo.** Rows describing a single company are pipeline entries that have wandered into the strategy sheet.
6. **Decay awareness.** For each metric, state what happens to it when the incentive stops. If the answer is "it goes to zero," that is a fine answer, but the goal should then be framed as acquisition, and a separate retention row should exist.

## Relationship to the direct GTM plan

A partner is a persona too. Before committing a row, run the partner segment through the same short chain from `references/icp-personas.md`: what is their pain, what initiative are they running, who decides, who pays, what is the route. A BD conversation with someone who cannot approve an integration and does not control a budget is a coffee, not a partnership.
