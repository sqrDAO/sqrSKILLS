## Persona tiering

Four slots, and the tiering is the whole point. A list of four equally weighted personas is a list of zero personas.

| Slot | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| A | Must win | If you cannot sell to this persona, the company does not work. Every default in the product, the pricing, and the copy serves them. |
| B | Tier 1 | Reliably closeable, adjacent to A. Second in line for roadmap and messaging. |
| C | Tier 2 | Closeable with effort or a variant of the offer. Take the inbound, do not chase. |
| D | Tier 3 | Occasionally buys. Log the pattern; do not build for them. |

If a founder cannot name Persona A without hedging, stop the rest of the exercise and work on that.

## The persona record

Each persona has three blocks. Fill all three or the record does not qualify anyone.

**Company layer** (which accounts to target)
- Industry
- Revenue (a band, not a point estimate)
- Employee size
- Countries

**Human layer** (who inside the account)
- Job title (plus the two or three title variants you will actually see)
- Work experience (years, and the kind of prior role that predicts receptiveness)
- Education background (include only if it changes the message)

**Others**
- Relevance: one sentence on why this persona, in this company profile, is the one who feels the pain you solve. This is the field people skip and it is the field that carries the record.

Apply the "so what" test to every attribute. An attribute that changes no message, channel, or qualifying question is decoration.

## Getting the persona's attention

Three questions, per persona, answered from evidence rather than intuition. The evidence comes from asking five customers, not from a brainstorm.

1. **Interaction preferences.** What are the best ways to reach this persona? Cold email, warm intro, conference hallway, Telegram, LinkedIn DM, community Discord, RFP portal. Note both the channel that works and the channel they say they prefer, because these differ.
2. **Content types preferred.** What form of content do they consume when learning about solutions in this area? Benchmark reports, peer case studies, technical docs, a live demo, a spreadsheet model they can run themselves, a podcast.
3. **Content sources.** Which news, data, and insight sources do they use to stay current in this area? Name specific publications, newsletters, analysts, and communities. "Twitter" is not an answer. "Sixteen accounts, of which these four" is.

Together these three answers produce the channel plan. Without them, the channel plan is whichever channel the founder personally enjoys.

## Qualifying questions: your solution

Answer these once, for the company, before persona work.

1. Describe the problem your solution is solving. State it as something that is currently costing someone money, time, or risk. If the problem statement contains your product name, rewrite it.
2. Describe your solution.
3. Are there existing solutions in the market that solve the same problem?
   - **3a. If yes:** how are you different from what already exists? Differentiation must be something a buyer can verify, not an adjective.
   - **3b. If no:** why do you think that is? Push here. The three real answers are: the market is too new, the market is too small, or the incumbent solution is "do nothing in a spreadsheet." All three have consequences. "Nobody thought of it" is not among them.
4. How is your solution different from whatever already exists in the market? (Yes, this overlaps with 3a. It is asked twice on purpose, once against named competitors and once against the status quo. Buyers choose the status quo more often than they choose a competitor.)

## Qualifying questions: your target market

1. How much revenue would they roughly need for it to make sense for them to purchase your solution? Work backwards from your price and a defensible ROI multiple.
2. How are they earning their revenue right now?
   - **2a.** Given their business model, does it make sense for them to purchase or partner with your service? A company whose cost structure does not contain the line item you shrink will not buy no matter how much they like you.
3. Why would they partner with or purchase your service or product?
4. How much are they likely willing to spend? Or, for partnerships, what kind of partnership model would they be interested in?
5. Can they afford your service or product? Willingness and ability are different questions and the second one is the one that kills deals in month four.

## Qualifying questions: your buyer's persona

1. What pain points drove this persona to seek your solution?
2. What motivates this persona to seek improvements in this area of pain? Personal motivation, not corporate. Promotions, avoided blame, and reclaimed evenings close deals that ROI decks do not.
3. What does your solution help this persona to enable? Frame as a new capability, not a removed annoyance.
4. Describe the initiative they would be taking that leads them to seeking your solution. Buyers do not buy categories, they buy line items inside an initiative that already has a name and a sponsor. Find the initiative's name.
5. **Are they Decision Makers?**
   - 5a. If yes: who else do they need to convince or present to?
   - 5b. If no: who do they report to, and who is the Decision Maker?
     - 5bi. What is the route to the Decision Maker? Name the path (champion escalation, exec sponsor intro, board deck, procurement) and the trigger that opens it.
6. **Are they Purse Holders (financial approver)?**
   - 6a. If yes: what do they need to see in order to allocate budget to your solution?
   - 6b. If no: who is the Purse Holder, and what do they need to see to allocate budget?
     - 6bi. What is the route to Budget? Existing budget line, reallocation, new budget request, or unbudgeted discretionary spend. These four have completely different timelines, and the fourth is the only one that can close this quarter.

The pain-to-budget chain, restated: pain, initiative, decision maker, purse holder, budget route. A persona missing any link is a user, not a buyer. That is fine, users matter, but do not forecast revenue against them.

## Worked example

**Persona A, must win.**

*Company:* Industry: mid-market Southeast Asian remittance operators. Revenue: $5M to $50M annual. Employee size: 30 to 200. Countries: Vietnam, Philippines, Indonesia.

*Persona:* Job title: Head of Payments Operations (variants: VP Operations, Treasury Lead). Work experience: 8 to 15 years, usually ex-bank correspondent banking. Education: omitted, changes nothing.

*Relevance:* Owns the correspondent-banking cost line and personally fields the escalations when a corridor settles late. Feels the pain daily and reports the number monthly.

*Pain:* Correspondent fees plus three-day settlement on a corridor where competitors now quote same-day.

*Initiative:* "Corridor cost reduction FY26," sponsored by the CFO.

*Decision maker:* No. Reports to COO. Route: champion escalation once a pilot shows a measured basis-point saving.

*Purse holder:* No. CFO. Needs a signed pilot result and a compliance sign-off. Route to budget: reallocation from the existing correspondent-fee line, which means the ask is a swap rather than a new spend, which means it can close inside a quarter.

Note how each field constrains the next. That is what a filled persona record is for.
