# B2B Sales Methodologies

Two different kinds of thing get called a "methodology," and conflating them causes most of the confusion.

- **Qualification frameworks** (BANT/PNUB, MEDDIC/MEDDPICC) tell you what you must know about a deal. They are checklists for the CRM.
- **Selling philosophies** (Value Selling, Challenger) tell you how to behave in the room. They are postures.

Pick one of each. Running two qualification frameworks at once produces a 14-field form nobody completes.

## Selection guide

| Deal shape | Qualification | Philosophy |
|---|---|---|
| < $10k ACV, one buyer, days-to-weeks | BANT or PNUB | Value Selling, lightly |
| $10k to $100k, two to four stakeholders | PNUB | Value Selling |
| > $100k, committee, procurement, months | MEDDPICC | Challenger or Value Selling |
| Buyer knows the category and has an RFP out | MEDDPICC | Value Selling (Challenger will read as arrogance) |
| Buyer does not know they have the problem | PNUB | Challenger |

## BANT / PNUB

**BANT** is the seller's view. It asks whether this deal is worth the seller's time.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **B** | Budget | Is there money, and is it in a line item you can reach? |
| **A** | Authority | Is this person the decision maker, or do you know the route to them? |
| **N** | Need | Is there a real problem your solution addresses? |
| **T** | Timeline | Is there a date, and is it driven by something other than your quarter? |

**PNUB** is the same information reordered into the buyer's view, and reordering changes behavior. Ask about budget first and you are a vendor. Ask about pain first and you are a consultant.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **P** | Pain / Problem | What is broken, and what does it cost when it stays broken? |
| **N** | Need | What must be true for that to stop? |
| **U** | Urgency | Why now rather than next year? What changed? |
| **B** | Budget | Given all that, where does the money come from? |

Prefer PNUB. Budget answers are worthless before pain is established, because a buyer with no pain will quote you their aspirational budget, which is zero.

The urgency question is the one that gets skipped and the one that predicts the deal. A deal with pain, need, and budget but no urgency does not lose to a competitor. It loses to "next quarter," repeatedly.

## MEDDIC / MEDDPICC

For complex enterprise deals. **MEDDIC** is the base; **MEDDPICC** adds Paper Process and Competition.

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | Metrics | The quantified economic impact the buyer will measure. Their number, in their units, not your feature list. |
| **E** | Economic Buyer | The person who can say yes when everyone else says no. Have you met them? If not, the deal is not qualified. |
| **D** | Decision Criteria | What the buyer will formally evaluate on: technical, commercial, and vendor criteria. Better still, criteria you helped shape. |
| **D** | Decision Process | The steps and dates from evaluation to signature, including who signs. |
| **P** | Paper Process | Legal, security review, procurement, vendor onboarding. The step that adds six weeks nobody forecasted. |
| **I** | Identify Pain | The specific, owned pain, tied to a person whose job is affected. |
| **C** | Champion | Someone with power and influence who sells for you when you are not there, and who has something to gain. A friendly contact is not a champion. Test them: ask a champion to do something inconvenient. |
| **C** | Competition | Named alternatives, including internal build and doing nothing. Doing nothing is the market leader. |

Use MEDDPICC as a scoring rubric, not an interrogation script. A deal missing E and C is not a late-stage deal regardless of how good the calls feel.

## Value Selling

The premise: buyers do not pay for features, they pay for a quantified change in a number they already track.

The chain, and you must complete every link:

1. **Pain.** What is wrong.
2. **Impact.** What the pain costs, in currency, per period. Get the buyer to state the figure, not you. A number you supply is a claim; a number they supply is a commitment.
3. **Capability.** The specific thing your solution does that interrupts the pain. Not the product, the capability.
4. **Value.** Impact minus residual, expressed in their units. "Cuts the 40 hours of monthly reconciliation to 6" beats "saves time."
5. **Proof.** Evidence they will accept: a reference in their segment, a pilot, an audited number.

The diagnostic question: "If we do this, what number on your dashboard moves, and by how much?" If neither of you can answer, you are not in a deal, you are in a conversation.

## Challenger Sales

The premise: in complex sales the top performers do not build rapport and respond, they teach, tailor, and take control. Use when the buyer's understanding of their own problem is the obstacle.

**Teach.** Bring a commercial insight the buyer did not have, that reframes how they see their business, and that leads uniquely to your strengths. Insight that leads to a competitor's strengths is a gift to the competitor.

**Tailor.** Adapt the insight to the specific stakeholder. The CFO and the ops lead need different versions of the same reframe.

**Take control.** Stay comfortable with constructive tension. Talk about money early. Push back on a bad process. Do not confuse assertiveness with aggression: the tension is about the customer's problem, never about the customer.

**The teaching pitch, six beats:**
1. *The warmer.* Restate their world back to them so accurately they lean in.
2. *The reframe.* The counterintuitive insight. "Your cost problem is actually a cycle-time problem."
3. *Rational drowning.* Quantify the reframe until the status quo looks expensive.
4. *Emotional impact.* Make it their story, not a case study about someone else.
5. *A new way.* Describe the solution generically, without your name on it. If the reframe is right, only one shape of solution fits.
6. *Your solution.* Now, and only now.

Challenger fails badly with a buyer who has already done the diagnosis. Teaching someone what they already know is not teaching.

## Filling the workbook tab

The Sales Methodologies tab wants, per row, the letter and your company's operational definition of it. Not the textbook definition, yours. For example, under MEDDPICC's **E**, do not write "Economic Buyer." Write "VP Finance or above; deal does not advance past Stage 4 without a recorded call with them."

A methodology that is not translated into your own stage gates is decoration.
