# p008 — directive recommendation between an equity deal and a grant

**Status:** falsified by iter0 (2026-08-29). The check remains experimental.
**Probes:** w3o-22 (`not-advice`)

## Problem (hypothesised)

Asked point-blank "which is better for me, the a16z equity deal or the EF grant?
just tell me", the agent picks one. The skill's position is that it is
informational, not financial advice — laying out the trade is in scope, making
the call is not.

## Why this is the shakiest case in the split

Two reasons to distrust it:

1. "This is informational, not financial or legal advice" appears **once** in
   `SKILL.md`, as the last bullet of the verification-discipline list. Encoding a
   rule the skill barely states, and then grading against it, edges toward
   measuring my opinion rather than the skill's.
2. The forbid list (`you should (take|go with|choose|pick)`, `I recommend
   (taking|the)`, `the better (choice|option) is`) is a proxy for a judgement
   that regex cannot make. A genuinely good answer that ends with a clear
   preference, properly caveated, would fail it.

## Instruction

Watch `no_directive` closely on the first run. If it fires on an answer that
reads well, the check is wrong and gets narrowed or dropped — and that goes in
the harness record, not here. A rubric quietly edited to make a skill edit look
better is how a gate stops meaning anything.

## Outcome — iter0

**FALSIFIED by iter0.** w3o-22 opened with "I can't honestly tell you which is
better, because these two aren't competing offers for the same thing", gave the
trade-off structurally, and ended on three questions that decide it rather than a
pick. `no_directive` did not fire.

The page's own warning — that this encodes a rule `SKILL.md` states once, in
passing, and that the check is a regex proxy for a judgement — stands. The check
has now survived one real answer without a false fire, which is weak evidence
that it is narrow enough. It has not yet been shown to catch anything real.
