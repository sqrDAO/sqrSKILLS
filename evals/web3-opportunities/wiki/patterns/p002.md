# p002 — non-dilutive queried when the user would accept mixed

**Status:** falsified by iter0 (2026-08-29).
**Probes:** w3o-03, w3o-04 (`dilution-exact`, `dilution-mixed-explicit`)

## Problem (hypothesised)

`--dilution non-dilutive` is exact-match: it excludes the two `mixed` entries
(Colosseum Hackathon, Founders Inc.) by design. A user who says "non-dilutive,
though I'd consider a small equity slice" is asking for `non-dilutive,mixed`.
The prediction is that the agent hears the first clause, passes
`--dilution non-dilutive`, and silently drops the two programmes the second
clause was there to include.

The inverse failure is w3o-03: a strict non-dilutive query, answered by listing
Colosseum among the non-dilutive options — the filter ran but the answer ignored
it.

## Suspected root cause

`SKILL.md` documents the semantics as a property of the flag ("`--dilution` is
exact-match: `non-dilutive` does NOT auto-include `mixed` — ask for `mixed`
explicitly when relevant"). It is written for someone reading the reference, not
as a decision the agent has to make while parsing a hedged sentence. "When
relevant" is doing a lot of unspecified work.

## What would falsify it

The agent widens the facet on w3o-04 unprompted. The wording of the gotcha would
then be adequate and the two cases become regression tests.

## Note

w3o-03's forbid list is claim-shaped on purpose — `colosseum[^.]{0,80}non-dilutive`
rather than `colosseum`. An answer that names Colosseum *in order to explain why
it was excluded* is a good answer, and a bare phrase forbid would fail it.

## Outcome — iter0

**FALSIFIED by iter0.** Both probes passed. w3o-04's hedged prompt ("though
I'd also consider a program that takes a small slice of equity") produced
`--dilution non-dilutive,mixed` on the first call, and the answer separated the
two mixed entries into their own section explaining what "mixed" costs. w3o-03
ran the strict filter and explicitly said why Colosseum and Founders Inc. were
excluded, then offered to include them.

"Ask for `mixed` explicitly when relevant" turns out to be adequate. The gotcha
is documented well enough.
